# Description

**55 completely new drawings, plus 1 bonus activity.** None of them repeat Volume 1, so together the two volumes give you 111 different activities, each at two levels of difficulty.

Drawing helps children develop creativity and fine motor skills, while tracing pictures and words provides additional handwriting practice. These activities help children focus, improve hand-eye coordination, and gradually build confidence in both drawing and writing.

That is why each activity in this resource follows four simple stages:

**1. Step-by-Step Drawing.** The picture is broken down into small, easy-to-follow steps. Children can see where to start and what to draw next. This helps them stay focused, follow the drawing process, and gradually become more confident in their drawing skills.

**2. Trace the Picture.** Children trace the lines and shapes of the completed picture, developing fine motor skills and hand-eye coordination.

**3. Draw and Color Independently.** Children draw the picture on their own, choose their colors, and can add their own details, encouraging independence and creativity.

**4. Trace and Write the Word.** Children first trace the word and then write it independently, getting additional handwriting practice while reinforcing the spelling of the word.

The resource includes 55 activities plus 1 bonus activity, covering a wide variety of themes: animals, bugs and little creatures, sea life, fantasy, vehicles, sports and hobbies, things, nature, and food. The drawings vary in complexity, allowing teachers to choose activities that best fit their students.

Each activity is provided at two difficulty levels:

**Level 1.** The picture is completed in **4 steps**. At each step, the part already drawn appears in light gray, while the new lines to add appear in black. The drawing area includes a light-gray starting shape, so the child continues the picture and adds the remaining details.

**Level 2.** The same picture is completed in **more steps - from five to eight, depending on the drawing**. Children begin with the simplest shapes and gradually add details. There is no starting shape in the drawing area, so children complete the entire picture independently.

This gives teachers **112 ready-to-print worksheets** at different difficulty levels. Level 1 can be used at the beginning of the school year, with a gradual transition to Level 2 as students become more confident and ready to work more independently.

The two levels also make it easier to work with students at different skill levels without having to find or modify separate activities for each child. The whole class can work on the same picture while the teacher chooses the appropriate level for each student.

No additional prep is required: simply choose a page, print it, and use it in class.

These activities work well for morning work, centers, independent work, and early finishers.
